SCIENTIFIC METHODOLOGY & LIMITATIONS

Downhill Flow Routing (D8) & Terrain Model

Copernicus WorldDEM-30 Hydrologic Conditioning

What is the D8 Algorithm?

The Deterministic Eight-Node (D8) algorithm routes surface water drainage from each elevation cell to its steepest down-slope neighbor among the 8 adjacent grid cells. In HydroViz, it traces gravity drainage vectors across the Copernicus WorldDEM-30 terrain grid.

⚠️ Critical Scientific Caveat: Terrain Runoff vs Historical Floodwater

A terrain-derived runoff path is NOT proof of the route taken by actual historical floodwater. Catastrophic Himalayan floods (such as Melamchi and Bhote Koshi) involve glacial lake outburst floods (GLOF), landslide-dam breaches, extreme sediment bulking, and debris damming at narrow gorges. A D8 trace reflects bare-earth gravity runoff, not debris-choked surge hydraulics or historical inundation.

Himalayan Terrain Parameters

  • Slope Cutoff (Default 20°): Steep Himalayan walls create extreme radar shadow and layover in Sentinel-1 SAR imagery. Slopes exceeding this cutoff are masked out to eliminate false standing-water detections on cliffs.
  • HAND Buffer (Default 15m): Height Above Nearest Drainage measures vertical elevation above the riverbed. Flood detection is constrained to low-lying riparian terraces, preventing false alarms on high ridges.
  • Cutoff Scenarios (Strict vs Conservative):
    • Strict: Classifies roads and hamlets as severed only when intersecting confirmed standing floodwater.
    • Conservative: Also factors in surrounding debris scour fields and erosion zones, accounting for damaged culverts and rock deposits.

Comparison against Copernicus EMS EMSR927
This benchmark compares HydroViz's automated multimodal pipeline against the Copernicus Emergency Management Service (EMS) activation EMSR927 for the 26 August 2026 flood event. Per Section 2 & 8 rules, EMSR927 data was strictly isolated as check-only and never fed into pipeline tuning or model training.

Methodology Differences & Limitations:
• Exposure vs Structural Damage: HydroViz measures physical exposure (spatial overlay with flood & debris mask). EMSR927 counts visible structural failure through manual photo-interpretation.
• Terrain Layover & Shadow: Steep Himalayan gorge slopes (>20°) cause radar shadow and foreshortening, which HydroViz transparently masks out using Copernicus DEM.
